# The potato: the structural element of the Spanish tortilla

The potato (*Solanum tuberosum*) is the cornerstone and structural backbone of the Spanish tortilla.

Far more than a simple ingredient, it defines the three-dimensional architecture, water retention, and mouthfeel of the dish. Although the canonical recipe requires only four components — potatoes, eggs, extra virgin olive oil, and salt — the physics and chemistry of this tuber mark the boundary between an extraordinary tortilla and a dry or lackluster result.

The choice of agricultural variety, cutting geometry, the balance between amylose and amylopectin, and thermal technique during olive oil confiture completely transform its interaction with egg proteins.

---

## The sacred triad of the tortilla

The Spanish tortilla relies on the physicochemical equilibrium of its three core pillars:

| Component | Core Function | Physicochemical Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Potato | Structure + Starch | Provides internal framework, lipid absorption, and starch gelation |
| Egg | Coagulation + Creaminess | Provides protein matrix (ovalbumin) and natural emulsifiers (lecithin) |
| Olive Oil | Thermal Medium + Flavor | Uniformly transfers heat during confit cooking without burning |

Specifically, the potato contributes:

- The solid volumetric foundation of the dish.
- Internal tender texture contrasting with the delicate outer golden skin.
- Creaminess enhanced by thermal starch gelatinization.
- Absorption capacity when warm potatoes soak in beaten egg mixture during resting.

## New potatoes versus mature (stored) potatoes

The physiological age of the tuber dramatically alters its pan performance:

- **New potatoes:** Higher water content (>82%) and lower starch (<16%). They hold firm shape but can release excess water during egg soaking, diluting the batter.
- **Mature / Stored potatoes:** Higher dry matter (>22%) and dense starch, guaranteeing superior gelatinization and optimal binding with egg proteins.

## Bactericidal standard and food safety

Every preparation method must strictly comply with food safety thresholds:

Reach an internal core temperature of **70°C for 2 minutes** or **63°C for 20 seconds** to eliminate foodborne pathogens such as *Salmonella*. Consume cooked tortilla within **4 hours** at ambient room temperature, or keep refrigerated below **8°C**.
