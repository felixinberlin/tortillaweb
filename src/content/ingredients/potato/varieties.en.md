# Potato varieties for Spanish tortilla

Selecting the agricultural cultivar is the first major decision that irreversibly determines the internal architecture of the Spanish tortilla.

Not all potatoes respond equally to slow confit submersed in low-temperature olive oil (130°C–140°C). Dry matter ratio, moisture-to-starch content, and cellular parenchymal structure dictate whether a potato will absorb beaten egg to form a uniform creamy batter or release free water and ruin cohesion.

---

## The three major culinary cultivars

In traditional Spanish gastronomy, three cultivars stand out for their predictable physicochemical performance:

### 1. Monalisa (Semi-early variety)
- **Composition:** Medium moisture (78–80%), balanced starch (18–20%).
- **Thermal performance:** Maintains optimal cellular flexibility during confit. Smooth skin and pale yellow flesh do not over-absorb oil.
- **Culinary outcome:** The gold standard for **classic traditional tortilla**. Provides silky creaminess without collapsing, melting smoothly into set egg curd.

### 2. Kennebec (Mid-to-late variety)
- **Composition:** High dry matter (20–22%), low reducing sugar content.
- **Thermal performance:** Highly resistant to oil breakdown, floury and soft texture after cooking with high flavor absorption.
- **Culinary outcome:** The iconic cultivar for **Betanzos-style tortilla** and Galician heritage recipes. Its ability to bind with liquid egg yolk creates a creamy flow and rich aroma.

### 3. Agria (Late variety)
- **Composition:** High starch level (21–23%), low moisture (<75%).
- **Thermal performance:** Reacts with a signature bright golden color. High starch forms a subtle outer crust while keeping an exceptionally tender core.
- **Culinary outcome:** Preferred for **rustic tavern-style tortillas**, where a distinct contrast between firm potato bite and creamy egg is desired.

---

## New potatoes versus mature (stored) potatoes

The physiological age of the tuber transforms its internal chemistry:

### New potatoes (early harvest)
- **Moisture:** >82% cellular water.
- **Starch:** <16% incomplete starch synthesis.
- **Effect on tortilla:** Retains firm shape, but high osmotic pressure releases free water during egg soaking, diluting the egg mixture.

### Mature / Stored potatoes (season harvest)
- **Moisture:** <76% water.
- **Starch:** >21% gelatinizable starch.
- **Effect on tortilla:** Optimal gelatinization. Soaks up egg without diluting it, forming a stable protein-lipid network.

## Food safety requirements

In all preparations, adhere strictly to bactericidal safety standards: reach an internal core temperature of **70°C for 2 minutes** or **63°C for 20 seconds**, consume within **4 hours** if stored at room temperature, or keep chilled below **8°C**.
