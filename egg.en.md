# Egg 🥚 — The Protein Matrix of the Spanish Tortilla

## /ingredients/egg/

The egg (*Gallus gallus domesticus*) is the protein heart and structural matrix of the Spanish tortilla.

Its role goes far beyond simply binding pieces of potato together: it acts as an emulsifying agent capable of integrating olive oil fat, the water contained in the potato, and the starch released during cooking.

The egg transforms humble ingredients into a complex preparation where structure, creaminess, and balance depend on a delicate relationship between chemistry and technique.

The freshness, temperature, proportion, and preparation method of the egg determine the defining characteristics of a tortilla: **juiciness, fluidity, texture, and food safety**.

---

# 🥚 1. The Role of Egg in the Tortilla

The egg performs four essential functions:

## Structure

Egg proteins form a three-dimensional network when exposed to heat. This structure holds the ingredients together and defines the firmness of the tortilla’s outer layer.

## Emulsion

The yolk contains lecithin and lipoproteins capable of combining elements that would normally separate: water and fat.

Thanks to this natural emulsion, olive oil, potato, and egg become a homogeneous and creamy mixture.

## Texture

The amount of egg determines the personality of the tortilla:

* More egg → a wetter, creamier, more fluid texture.
* Less egg → a firmer, denser tortilla.

## Flavor and Color

The yolk provides richness, golden color, and the characteristic fatty, silky sensation of a well-made tortilla.

---

# 🔬 2. Chemical Structure and Coagulation

The egg is composed of two main parts with different thermal behaviors: the white and the yolk.

## The Egg White (Albumen)

The egg white represents approximately 60% of the egg’s total weight and is mainly composed of water and proteins such as:

* Ovalbumin
* Conalbumin
* Lysozyme

It contains approximately 88% water.

Coagulation begins between **58 °C and 62 °C**.

During cooking, the proteins create a structural network that provides strength and supports the body of the tortilla.

## The Egg Yolk

The yolk contains:

* Lipids
* Lecithin
* Lipoproteins
* Fat-soluble vitamins

Its coagulation occurs approximately between **65 °C and 68 °C**.

It is responsible for:

* Emulsification.
* Golden color.
* Creamy mouthfeel.
* The liquid or *coulant* center found in lightly set tortillas.

---

# 🍳 3. The Correct Egg-Beating Technique

Egg for tortilla should not be treated like an ingredient in baking.

The objective is to combine white and yolk, not to incorporate air.

The traditional technique consists of:

* Breaking the yolk membrane.
* Mixing white and yolk gently.
* Avoiding foam formation.

Excessive beating introduces air bubbles and creates a fluffy texture similar to a soufflé or sponge cake, moving away from the dense, moist, creamy bite of traditional Spanish tortilla.

---

# 🟡 The Betanzos Method: Egg Without Previous Beating

The Betanzos school takes this approach to the extreme.

At restaurants such as Mesón O Pote, the traditional technique avoids even pre-beating the eggs.

The eggs are cracked directly and mixed with freshly cooked potatoes. The heat from the potatoes begins transforming the mixture before it reaches the pan.

The result is a tortilla where the yolk dominates the final texture: exceptionally creamy and fluid.

---

# 📊 4. Ratios: The Great Tortilla Schools

The relationship between potato and egg defines the culinary style.

| Style                       | Approximate Ratio                | Result                              |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Classic Spanish tortilla    | 1 egg per 100 g raw potato       | Balanced, juicy, and stable         |
| Betanzos style              | 12 eggs per 350 g potato         | Very fluid center dominated by yolk |
| Competition-style tortillas | Whole eggs plus additional yolks | Extremely creamy texture            |

There is no universally correct ratio: each school pursues a different experience.

# 🌡️ 5. Temperature and the Resting Rule

## Avoiding Thermal Shock

Eggs should preferably be used at room temperature.

Adding refrigerator-cold eggs to hot potatoes can cause:

* A sudden temperature drop.
* Irregular coagulation.
* Poor integration between egg and potato.

## Resting the Mixture

After draining the hot confited potatoes (approximately 60 °C to 70 °C), many techniques recommend resting them together with the egg for a few minutes.

During this time:

* The potato absorbs part of the egg.
* Gelatinized starch slightly thickens the mixture.
* A more stable emulsion develops.

This step helps create a juicy tortilla that maintains its texture when sliced.

---

# 🛡️ 6. Egg Safety and Food Control

A lightly set tortilla requires a balance between texture and safety.

The main risk associated with eggs is the bacteria **Salmonella Enteritidis**.

Safety depends on:

* Proper egg storage.
* Good handling practices.
* Temperature control.

Food regulations for professional kitchens require sufficient thermal treatment to reduce microbiological risk. When adequate cooking cannot be guaranteed, pasteurized liquid egg products are used.

Lightly cooked tortillas made with fresh eggs require particular attention because they retain more moisture and have a lower internal temperature.

---

# 🧫 7. Salmonella Survival on Kitchen Surfaces

Food safety does not depend only on the egg itself, but also on preparation surfaces and utensils.

Salmonella can survive for extended periods depending on the material:

* Stainless steel: several days under favorable conditions.
* Food-grade plastics: can retain microorganisms for longer periods due to their structure.
* Wood: its porous nature can make complete cleaning more difficult.

Essential practices include:

* Proper cleaning.
* Separation between raw and cooked foods.
* Replacement of damaged utensils.

---

# 🐔 8. Choosing Eggs for a Spanish Tortilla

## Freshness

A fresh egg provides:

* Firmer egg white.
* Better structural capacity.
* Lower water release.

## Size

Egg size directly affects the final potato-to-egg ratio.

## Yolk Color

A deeper orange yolk does not necessarily indicate higher quality: it mainly depends on the hen’s diet.

## European Egg Production Code

The first number indicates the production system:

* 0 — Organic
* 1 — Free-range
* 2 — Barn-raised
* 3 — Cage system

# 🌱 9. Vegan and Egg-Free Alternatives

Replacing egg is one of the greatest challenges in vegan tortilla making because the goal is not only to imitate flavor: the structure, emulsification, and texture must also be recreated.

The most common combination uses:

## Chickpea Flour

Provides:

* Protein.
* Yellow color.
* Setting ability.

## Tapioca Starch

Provides:

* Elasticity.
* Gel-like texture.
* A mouthfeel similar to egg yolk.

These techniques allow the creation of convincing vegan tortillas, although the unique chemistry of egg remains difficult to reproduce completely.

---

# 🍽️ Egg as the Identity of the Tortilla

Spanish tortilla is not simply cooked potato.

It is a hot emulsion where egg determines the final personality:

* In Betanzos, egg is the absolute protagonist.
* In the classic tortilla, it seeks balance with potato.
* In modern interpretations, it becomes a technical tool.
* In vegan versions, it represents the greatest substitution challenge.

Egg is the ingredient that transforms a simple combination of potato and olive oil into one of Spain’s most recognizable culinary creations.
