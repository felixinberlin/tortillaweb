202. **Sanchidrián's Day of Leftovers:** The day following Sanchidrián’s festival, March 26, is officially celebrated as the "Day of Leftovers" (*día de las sobras*), where families gather to finish any cold tortilla [120].

206. **Peraleda's Easter Kid Chops:** In Peraleda de San Román (Cáceres), Easter Sunday potato tortillas are traditionally paired with local kid goat chops (*chuletillas de cabrito*) cooked outdoors [122].

210. **The Checkered Napkin Prestige:** In Torija, carrying your countryside lunch wrapped in a checkered cloth napkin (*servilleta de tela de cuadros*) is said to give your group extra "prestige" (*caché*) [123].

## 🍇 II. Zamora & Madrid Regional Games & Feasts (Facts 246-255)

246. **Zamora's Grammatical Rule:** In the Benavente and Los Valles region of Zamora, the Sunday before Palm Sunday is strictly referred to as "Tortillero" (as a proper noun, without the article "el" or "día de la") [129].
247. **Zamora's Field Blessings:** The first half of Zamora’s Tortillero is deeply agricultural, consisting of a church mass, a procession, and a formal blessing of the local crops and fields to ask for good harvests [129].
248. **Zamora's Wine Cave Feasts:** The second half of Tortillero is spent inside traditional underground wine caves (*bodegas*) sharing tortillas and grilled meats [129].
249. **Zamora's Clay Pot Chicken:** Along with the tortilla, a classic Tortillero wine cave meal in Zamora must include chicken slow-cooked in a traditional clay pot (*pollo en tartera de barro*) [129].
250. **The Lost Custom of "Tortillín":** The traditional Zamoran custom of "Tortillín"—where friends gathered the Monday after Tortillero to eat up the leftovers—is slowly disappearing from the region [130].
251. **Morales del Rey's San Marcos Shift:** In the Zamoran town of Morales del Rey, the traditional Tortillero field blessing and wine cave feast is celebrated on April 25 (San Marcos) instead of the Sunday before Palm Sunday [131].
252. **Soto del Real's La Maya Crowning:** In Soto del Real (Madrid), the May 3 spring picnic is celebrated alongside dulzaina music and the crowning of a local girl as "La Maya" (Reina de la Primavera) [168].
253. **Soto del Real's Peaches in Syrup:** Soto del Real's traditional spring picnic menu always pairs potato tortillas with a dessert of rice pudding (*arroz con leche*) and sweet peaches in syrup [168].
254. **Hoyo de Manzanares's Sweet Aniseed Races:** In Hoyo de Manzanares (Madrid), the year's festivities kickoff on February 3 with the children's "correr la rosca" game, where kids race to win large sweet buns baked with lard, aniseeds, and confits [167].
255. **The Three Roscas Categories:** Historically, the Hoyo de Manzanares town hall funded three separate rosca races: one for married men (*casados*), one for single men (*solteros*), and one for children (*chicos*) [167].

275. **Teresa Cuerda's Ingredient Selection:** Teresa Cuerda of Casa Dani (Madrid) points out that after 30 years of making tortillas, the key remains selecting ingredients carefully, noting that even different eggs can vary significantly in water content [258].

## 🍳 IV. Other Spanish Egg Traditions (Facts 276-290)

276. **Arroz con Huevo Cuban Variations:** While arroz con huevo (rice with fried egg) is a beloved simple dish in Spain, it is equally popular as a comfort staple in Cuba, Colombia, and across Latin America [238].
277. **Spain's Arroz con Huevo Sofrito:** In Spain, arroz con huevo is typically upgraded and served with a thick sofrito sauce made with tomato paste, onions, and garlic [11].
278. **Andalusia's Baked Gypsy Eggs:** Andalusia’s huevos a la flamenca (Gypsy/Flamenco eggs) are baked inside a clay pot topped with tomatoes, peppers, onions, ham, and chorizo until the whites set and yolks remain runny [11].
279. **Asturias's Tortos Asturianos:** Rural Asturias is famous for tortos asturianos, which features a pan-fried corn and wheat flour fritter topped with sweet-and-tangy ground pork (*picadillo*) and a fried egg [11].
280. **The Star Shape of Huevos Estrellados:** When the runny yolks of huevos rotos (broken eggs) are pierced and cut over a bed of french fries, they frequently take on a star shape, which is why the dish is also called huevos estrellados [11].
281. **Murcia's Zucchini Zarangollo:** Murcia’s classic zarangollo consists of soft scrambled eggs cooked with thinly sliced zucchini, onions, and sometimes potatoes, served hot alongside a glass of wine [11, 306].
282. **Asturia's Sweet Apple Omelette:** In Asturias, home cooks prepare tortilla de manzana, a sweet-savory dessert omelette made with eggs and thinly sliced local apples [11].
283. **Spain's Artichoke Wedges:** A traditional national dish across Spain is the tortilla de alcachofas (artichoke omelette), sliced into thick wedges and served as a shared tapas plate [11, 283].
284. **Béchamel-Coated Deep-Fried Eggs:** Spain’s extraordinary huevos a la béchamel consists of hard-boiled eggs coated in thick béchamel sauce, rolled in breadcrumbs, deep-fried, and served with aioli [11].
285. **Pisto con Huevo Clay Bowl:** Pisto con huevo features a rich zucchini, onion, tomato, and bell pepper stew (similar to ratatouille) served inside a clay bowl and topped with a sunny-side-up egg [213].
286. **Priorat's Cod and Bean Gravy:** Catalonia’s Priorat region is famous for truita amb suc, a rich stew containing a bean-and-spinach stuffed omelette cooked alongside chunks of codfish in a thick garlic-paprika gravy [213].
287. **Truita amb Suc's Leftover Origins:** Truita amb suc originally began as humble peasant food designed as an ingenious way to use up leftover kitchen ingredients [213].
288. **New York's Tomiño Restaurant:** Chef Lucía Freitas highlights that New York’s Tomiño Restaurant successfully sells authentic, runny Galician Tortilla de Betanzos to highly enthusiastic New Yorkers [180].
289. **Freitas's Ingredient Standard:** Chef Lucía Freitas attributes the magic of the Betanzos tortilla to the high quality of the farm eggs and the specific taste of Galician potatoes [180].
290. **Cider House Bacalao Tortilla:** In Basque cider houses (*sagardotegis*), the traditional menu always includes a Bacalao (salt cod) tortilla, cooked with onions and parsley until extremely juicy [221, 290].
