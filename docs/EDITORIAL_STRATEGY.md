# Tortilla Website — Content Strategy & Editorial Intelligence

## 1. The purpose of the content

Tortilla de Patatas is not a recipe website.

It should become a **living encyclopedia and cultural map of the tortilla de patatas**.

Content should cover:

* recipes
* ingredients
* techniques
* science
* history
* people
* restaurants
* regions
* factions
* controversies
* records
* traditions
* variations
* food culture
* modern gastronomy
* interactive experiments

The site should make visitors think:

> "I came here for a tortilla recipe and somehow ended up learning why Spain argues about onions."

That progression is intentional.

---

## 2. Content principles

### Be specific

Prefer:

> "Why do potatoes behave differently when fried at different temperatures?"

over:

> "The science of cooking potatoes."

Prefer concrete questions, historical events, people, places and techniques.

### Every page needs a reason to exist

Do not create pages merely because a taxonomy exists.

A page should provide at least one of:

* useful knowledge
* historical evidence
* culinary technique
* scientific explanation
* cultural context
* controversy
* comparison
* entertainment
* practical cooking value

### Avoid generic food-blog writing

Do NOT fill pages with:

* generic introductions
* "Spain is famous for..."
* "This delicious dish..."
* repetitive descriptions
* meaningless SEO paragraphs
* obvious statements
* artificial conclusions

The site should feel more like an **encyclopedia written by people who are obsessed with tortilla** than a commercial food blog.

---

## 3. The editorial voice

The voice should be:

**knowledgeable + curious + slightly irreverent + precise**

The website may be playful, especially around factions and tortilla controversies.

But facts must remain facts.

Good:

> "The onion debate is less a culinary question than a Spanish constitutional crisis conducted over a frying pan."

Bad:

> "OMG! You won't believe what happens when you add onion!"

Humor should reinforce the subject, not replace information.

---

## 4. Separate FACT from LEGEND from OPINION

This is extremely important.

When discussing tortilla history:

### FACT

Use when supported by reliable historical documentation.

### LEGEND

Use explicitly when discussing stories such as:

* General Zumalacárregui
* the invention by an anonymous Navarrese woman
* Portuguese prisoners
* other traditional origin stories

Never present a famous culinary legend as established historical fact merely because it is repeated frequently.

### OPINION / TRADITION

Use when discussing:

* onion vs no onion
* preferred cooking point
* potato varieties
* pan size
* regional preferences
* chef opinions

The content model should preserve these distinctions where practical.

---

## 5. Content hierarchy

Think in layers.

### Level 1 — Pillar pages

Examples:

* Encyclopedia
* History
* Science
* Recipes
* Ingredients
* Techniques
* People
* Regions
* Factions
* Records

These are major knowledge hubs.

### Level 2 — Topic pages

Examples:

* Potato
* Egg
* Onion
* Betanzos tortilla
* Tortilla Paisana
* Tortilla de Sacromonte
* Tortilla with chips
* Salmonella and tortilla
* Tortilla in post-war Spain

### Level 3 — Deep pages

Examples:

* Potato starch and tortilla texture
* Why onions change sweetness
* Why a tortilla breaks during flipping
* The thermal profile of a tortilla
* Why Betanzos tortillas are unusually liquid
* The history of the tortilla without potatoes
* The chemistry of egg coagulation

Not every topic needs Level 3 content.

---

## 6. Generate CONTENT OPPORTUNITIES, not random pages

When reviewing the site, identify missing knowledge.

Look for:

### Missing questions

"What would a curious visitor naturally ask after reading this page?"

### Missing entities

Important:

* people
* restaurants
* regions
* ingredients
* techniques
* historical events
* tortilla styles

### Missing relationships

Ask:

> "What other page should this page naturally point to?"

For example:

Potato → starch → frying → texture → tortilla technique.

Betanzos → Galicia → Puristas → liquid center → food safety.

Onion → Concebollistas → caramelization → sweetness → faction debate.

---

## 7. Content should create a knowledge graph

Every important piece of content should connect to other content.

Avoid isolated articles.

A page about Betanzos should connect to:

* Betanzos region
* Puristas
* potato
* egg
* frying
* tortilla technique
* relevant people
* restaurants
* recipes
* history
* food safety

A page about onion should connect to:

* Concebollistas
* caramelization
* techniques
* recipes
* factions
* comparisons

Think in terms of **entities and relationships**, not blog posts.

---

## 8. Use the existing taxonomy system

The repository already contains taxonomies for concepts such as:

* factions
* ingredients
* people
* regions
* styles
* techniques
* difficulties

Do not create a new taxonomy when an existing one can represent the relationship.

If a new concept repeatedly appears across multiple pages, consider whether it deserves to become a first-class taxonomy/entity.

---

## 9. One fact can produce several useful pages

Do not duplicate the same paragraph everywhere.

Instead:

One fact → one authoritative page.

Other pages should reference it.

Example:

The history of the 1817 Navarra document belongs primarily in History.

A faction page can mention it briefly and link to the historical page.

A person page can reference the historical context.

This keeps the site maintainable and prevents contradictions.

---

## 10. Recipes are not the only content

A tortilla encyclopedia should have substantial non-recipe content.

Prioritize:

### History

* origins
* historical documents
* wartime stories
* poverty and food
* post-war adaptations
* industrialization
* modern tortilla culture

### Science

* egg coagulation
* potato starch
* frying temperature
* oil absorption
* water activity
* texture
* thermal gradients
* food safety
* resting

### Techniques

* cutting potatoes
* poaching
* frying
* mixing
* pan selection
* temperature control
* shaping
* flipping
* resting

### Culture

* onion debate
* regional identity
* tortilla competitions
* bar culture
* tortilla as tapa
* tortilla as home food
* tortilla as commercial product

### People

Create pages when a person has a meaningful relationship with the tortilla.

Do not create celebrity pages merely because the person once mentioned tortilla.

---

## 11. Create controversial content carefully

Controversy is valuable for this project.

Examples:

* Onion or no onion?
* Liquid or fully set?
* Is Betanzos the purest form?
* Are chips a legitimate tortilla ingredient?
* Can a vegan tortilla still be a tortilla?
* Is a tortilla cooked at restaurant temperatures actually safe?
* Where does "tortilla española" end and experimentation begin?

But distinguish:

**documented fact → expert opinion → popular belief → site interpretation**

Never blur them.

---

## 12. Content should sometimes be deliberately short

Not every entity needs 1,500 words.

A person may need:

* who they are
* why they matter
* their relationship with tortilla
* sources
* related pages

A region may need:

* local style
* ingredients
* history
* notable tortillas
* restaurants
* related people

A technique may need:

* what it does
* why it works
* how to perform it
* common mistakes
* science
* related recipes

Depth should follow the subject.

---

## 13. Avoid AI-content fingerprints

Never produce:

* repetitive three-part lists
* artificial "In conclusion..."
* empty rhetorical questions
* exaggerated claims
* invented quotations
* invented statistics
* invented historical connections
* fake expert consensus
* unnecessary adjectives

Do not write content merely to increase word count.

---

## 14. Sources and evidence

For factual claims, especially:

* historical dates
* scientific claims
* epidemiology
* statistics
* restaurant awards
* competitions
* quotations
* named people's opinions

the content pipeline should ideally store source information.

Where evidence is uncertain, explicitly mark it.

Never "upgrade" an uncertain claim into a fact.

If research material contains conflicting claims, preserve the uncertainty rather than silently choosing one.

---

## 15. Multilingual content

The site currently supports:

* Spanish
* English
* German

Spanish should normally be treated as the cultural source language.

Translations should preserve:

* factual meaning
* terminology
* tone
* faction names
* culinary vocabulary

Do not translate proper culinary concepts mechanically when the Spanish term is the recognized name.

Example:

"Tortilla de Betanzos" should remain identifiable as a named style.

---

## 16. SEO without SEO sludge

Every page should have a useful search intent.

Good:

> Why does tortilla de patatas break when flipping?

Bad:

> Best tortilla de patatas recipe tortilla Spanish omelette potato egg recipe Spain.

SEO should emerge from useful information.

Each page should have:

* unique title
* useful description
* canonical URL
* correct language
* hreflang
* meaningful headings
* internal links
* structured data where appropriate

Never add paragraphs solely because they contain keywords.

---

## 17. Content discovery loop

For every new content item ask:

1. What question does this answer?
2. Who would care?
3. What existing pages should link to it?
4. What pages should it link to?
5. Is it fact, legend, opinion or interpretation?
6. Does it deserve its own page?
7. Is there already a page answering this?
8. Can the information be represented better as an entity/taxonomy?
9. Does it need sources?
10. Should it exist in all three languages?

---

## 18. Prioritize content by value

Use this priority:

### P0 — Missing core knowledge

Content necessary to understand the site.

### P1 — Strong search / educational value

Questions people are likely to search for.

### P2 — Strong cultural value

Interesting regional, historical or social material.

### P3 — Fun / experimental

Records, curiosities, strange tortillas, unusual ingredients, debates.

### P4 — Nice to have

Minor facts and edge cases.

Do not spend development time generating dozens of P4 pages while important P0/P1 gaps remain.

---

## 19. Content audit behavior

When asked to improve content, do not immediately rewrite everything.

First identify:

* missing content
* duplicate content
* weak content
* unsupported claims
* poor internal linking
* taxonomy gaps
* translation gaps
* opportunities for deeper pages

Then propose the smallest useful change.

---

## 20. The ultimate editorial test

Before adding content, ask:

> "Would a person who is genuinely fascinated by tortilla de patatas find this interesting?"

If the answer is no, do not publish it.

The goal is not to make the site bigger.

The goal is to make it **hard to leave**.

---

## 21. The Mono-Food Benchmark & Radical Vertical Authority

When positioning `tortilladepatatas.org` against other single-dish institutions globally (AVPN pizza, Berlin döner museum, ramen museum, etc.):

1. **Facts, Not Fluff**: Never claim superiority using empty adjectives ("amazing", "unrivaled"). Always prove it with hard engineering and historical facts:
   - Zero-ad SVG payload (85 KB vs 14.8 MB commercial bloat).
   - Zero-login URL parametric state (the URL query is the database).
   - Archival primary sources (1798 Villanueva de la Serena) vs ubiquitous 1835 myths.
   - Precise thermodynamic denaturation curves vs vague "cook until golden" guidance.
2. **Respect the Counterparts**: Acknowledge genuine cultural value in other food monuments while dissecting their digital or structural vulnerabilities (e.g. physical lease overheads, static Web 1.0 layouts).

---

## 22. Organic Safety Integration vs. Boilerplate Clutter

Food safety (specifically **70°C for 2 minutes**, **63°C for 20 seconds**, and **4 hours** ambient limit) is non-negotiable science, but **repetition degrades editorial authority**.

- Integrate safety organically where cooking heat, egg physics, or food storage is actively discussed.
- Strictly avoid mechanically injecting identical disclaimer banners into cultural, biographical, humor, or community poll pages.
- Tone must be that of an expert culinary scientist in a laboratory notebook, not a bureaucratic warning label.
